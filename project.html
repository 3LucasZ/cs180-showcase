CS 180 · project 01

Images of the Russian Empire

Colorizing the Prokudin-Gorskii glass-plate collection — three exposures, one photograph, a century apart.

colorizationregistrationglass plates

Approach

Prokudin-Gorskii captured each scene three times through red, green, and blue filters. Nearly a century later, the three plates can be stacked into a color photograph — if the channels are aligned to within a few pixels. The whole project is registration.

Each channel is a crop of one negative, so I align the green and red plates against the blue one by searching a small displacement window and keeping the offset that minimizes the difference between overlapping pixels. For the larger plates, a coarse-to-fine pyramid search keeps the cost reasonable.

Results

Once the channels line up, the colors fall into place. Edges go clean where they were fringed, and the portrait reads as a photograph rather than a triple exposure.

The trickiest plates were the ones with large empty areas — sky, water, studio backdrops — where the alignment score goes flat and the search can drift. Cropping the evaluation to the center band of each plate fixes it.
